# What's new on `main-mac`

The `main-mac` branch runs VIctory on Apple Silicon. It starts from VIctory for macOS (Wine plus D3DMetal) and adds fixes from the Windows forks that already run the game well. Every change is tested on one MacBook Pro (M4 Pro, 48 GB) with the Xbox Series X profile.

This repository has source code only. It contains nothing from the game. Bring your own copy of the game and build the code yourself.

## Where it stands (28 November 2026)

- **Picture.** The sun now lights surfaces. Shadows have clean edges, rooms have fill light, characters and their clothes draw, and screen reflections reach the picture. The picture is close to the console at the checkpoints we compare against. Car interiors at night are lit, and at the boat the water simulation now runs, so the wake and spray show. It is not the same everywhere: night streets and rooms look flatter than on the console (our fill light's minimum level outweighs the lamps; the launcher's lower minimum brings back part of the contrast), distant trees and buildings appear 5 to 35 s late after a camera cut, and a black patch on water in one boat shot is still open. The far reflection pass, left out for a day because it drew blocks on water, runs again with its fix (see below). The late distant world is the game's streaming running slowly here, not missing detail levels; a larger map budget doesn't help.
- **Free roam.** From a free-roam save, the house, the street and the interiors look right, at about 2 fps. Some objects flicker black for one frame, mostly indoors. The main cause found so far has a fix in the launcher (see below); it is not yet checked by eye in free roam. The game caps each frame's time step at 1/15 s, so at 2 to 4 fps the world ran at 15 to 25% of normal speed; the launcher now allows 0.25 s. Held buttons (doors) worked; short taps (sprint, climb) were lost, and the launcher now keeps them. Neither fix is tried in play yet. In one play run the controller stopped responding soon after driving began; the launcher now restarts a stalled pad (see below).
- **Speed.** On the default launcher settings:

  | Place | Frames a minute | About |
  |---|---|---|
  | Prison checkpoint, still view | 174 to 204 | 2.9–3.4 fps |
  | Boat mission opening (cutscene on the boat) | 267 to 300 | 4.5–5.0 fps |
  | Boat mission opening, Series S profile, 1280x720 output | 448 (one run) | 7.5 fps |
  | Prison, 1920x1080 output | 179 | 3.0 fps |
  | Boat mission opening, 1920x1080 output | 295 | 4.9 fps |
  | Driving the boat (play), 1920x1080 output | about 120 | 2.0 fps |

  The prison row comes from runs on today's settings and counts the still view once the camera has settled; the first minute is slower (138 to 165 frames). The boat-opening rows count the cutscene that starts the mission, 30 to 90 s after the first world frame. It had slowed since this morning, and the cause is found: the launcher's wider flicker setting (`VICTORY_PLACED_AT_ONCE=all`) cost 16% there (231 to 257 frames against 286 to 296 with `=keep`, three runs each), so the launcher is back to `=keep`. The 28 November builds gave 267 to 300. On the newest launcher defaults (the far reflection pass back in, the null page and the compressed-texture casts, below) the fast prison runs give 174 to 180 frames a minute, against 185 to 192 earlier that day. On the Series S profile at 1280x720 output the GPU's share of each frame halves: the game's opening ran at 249 and 259 frames a minute against 173 on Series X, and our own preparing work becomes the larger part of the frame (about 120 ms). The 1920x1080 boat-opening row was measured with `=all`. Driving the boat is what you get in play: the water simulation runs and there are two to four times as many draws as in the cutscene. There Apple's Metal HUD shows the GPU busy for only 75 to 100 ms of a 500 ms frame, so most of the time goes to waiting, most likely between GPU submissions. Some runs go into a slow state partway through (see "Slow state" below): about a third of the runs at the prison and at the boat (25 of 84 on 28 November; 130 to 142 frames in the prison's first minute, against 168 to 198 in normal runs). The prison 1920x1080 row was measured before the census change below. At 1920x1080 the game's interface is drawn twice too large. `VICTORY_OUTPUT=3840x2160 VICTORY_RENDER_SCALE=0.5` keeps it right but is 30% slower in the cell. For about half of each frame, the thread that submits GPU work waits for the GPU. Preparing work and GPU time don't overlap yet.
- **Goal.** First, 5 fps on the Series X profile with the picture right. Then 10 fps at 1080p output. 30 fps is out of reach on this hardware with any code known today.

## Recent changes

- **Compressed-texture casts** (launcher default since 28 November, from VIctory for macOS #499). With `VICTORY_METAL_BC_CASTS=1` a block-compressed texture is viewed only as its sRGB partner, so the Metal texture-view abort seen on an M1 Max can't happen. Pictures in the prison cell and at the boat are the same; the cell ran at 322 ms a frame against 316 (within noise). `VICTORY_METAL_BC_CASTS=0` turns it off.
- **Series S profile in the launcher.** With `--console series-s`, `play-main-mac.sh` now shows the world's plane (`VICTORY_PRESENT_PLANE=0`) and draws the subtitles over it (`VICTORY_PRESENT_COMPOSITE=1`). Without them the window shows the interface plane only and the world looks black. The broken faces of [issue #9](https://github.com/halfwave50/VIctory-macOS-halfwave50/issues/9) (M5 Max) don't show here: the opening's faces are whole on this profile, with and without `VICTORY_UAV_SIZE_LEVEL0=1`.
- **Fixes from VIctory for macOS** (always on unless a switch is named): #615 (kostuch0) keeps a zero divisor out of the game's upscaler when the output is made small; #673 (civilwarinfrance) keeps the last complete save when a newer one is missing a file; #556 (Nick233x) ends a crash with `VICTORY_PATCH_WIDE`, where copied thread-storage calls jumped into nothing; #682 (Seabeast72042069) adds `VICTORY_STREAM_WATCH`, a log of what the streaming engine still waits for. Not measured apart here.
- **Far reflection pass back in** (launcher default since 28 November). The launcher no longer leaves out the ray-traced pass that drew blocks on water (`VICTORY_SKIP_PIPELINES=` is empty); it runs with its fix (`VICTORY_HALVES_WHOLE_WAVE=1`, see "Far reflection pass without blocks") and the narrower barrier list (`VICTORY_HALVES_WHOLE_WAVE_LDS=<ray programs>`, see "Narrower water fix"). Four boat runs showed clean water at the old defaults' speed, and a prison run ran at 303 ms a frame against 316 on the old defaults, with the same picture. `VICTORY_SKIP_PIPELINES=6d7fe7745ab9c6d8 VICTORY_HALVES_WHOLE_WAVE=0` goes back to the old way.
- **Null page** (launcher default since 28 November, from VIctory for macOS #634). With `VICTORY_NULL_PAGE=1` a read of the null page no longer counts as a missed page of game memory, so those views share one page instead of keeping copies (141 MB less). The picture is the same; the prison cell got no faster (168 and 179 frames a minute). `VICTORY_NULL_PAGE=0` turns it off.
- **From this repository's pull requests** (by dailytrap). [PR #4](https://github.com/halfwave50/VIctory-macOS-halfwave50/pull/4): three small speed-ups in the copy path (changed 4 KiB blocks are found anywhere in a chunk and scanned 8 bytes at a time, the page table is written in runs, extent levels merge in place); not measured apart. [PR #6](https://github.com/halfwave50/VIctory-macOS-halfwave50/pull/6): the library tests run in parallel. Our review of it found a lock-order deadlock and a few tests that shared state; both are fixed. From [PR #8](https://github.com/halfwave50/VIctory-macOS-halfwave50/pull/8) (by ottco), one commit so far: the maps of pending copies hash their address keys with one multiply instead of a general hash (4.4 ms a frame on the submitting thread in the author's profile); not measured apart here.
- **Zero-copy for the game's physical memory, first try** (off by default, `VICTORY_ALIAS_PHYSICAL`). About 95% of the megabytes still copied each frame lie in the game's physical memory pages, many of which it shows at more than one address. This change backs those pages with GPU memory the CPU can see too, so views of them need no copy. It passed every test, natively and under Wine, but the first game runs drew wrong pictures (the prison cell's frosted glass and walls missing, flat detail textures, a wrong shirt pattern at the boat), so it stays off. The debugging found a likely reason, and it reaches the defaults too (next item). But keeping those writes out didn't fix this change: with `VICTORY_ALIAS_PHYSICAL=gpu` the GPU still takes 4 to 6 s a frame and the picture washes out white, and a checking mode (`=verify`) ended in a crash. A simpler mode makes views of whole memory sections only (`=gpu-sections`, checked by `=verify-sections`). In the prison cell its check found no wrong binding, but almost every would-be view goes back to a copy (about 50 of 20,000 bindings a frame go through views), so it saves little as it stands (183 frames a minute against 174 on the defaults, one run each).
- **Writes from missing pages (fix off by default).** Some GPU work in the prison cell reads game memory that isn't mapped yet. With copies, its results are thrown away at the end of the frame; through shared memory they can't be. With today's zero-copy buffers about 20 MB a frame of such writes land in game memory (99 to 132 MB with the change above). It may be behind the rare watchdog crash (11 of 295 runs with zero-copy buffers, 4 of 256 without) and some one-frame flickers; not proven yet. The fix, `VICTORY_WITHHELD_COPIES=1`, gives a range of game memory a host copy once such a write has landed in it. In two prison runs almost nothing landed any more (0 MB in 52 of 54 frame lines), with the picture right and the same speed (185 and 187 frames a minute). Since the null page became a launcher default (above), nothing lands at the prison or the boat even without the fix, so it stays off as a net for other scenes. Whether the rare crash is gone will take many more runs.
- **Audio heap fix** (from VIctory for macOS #638, always on). The audio decoder could keep writing into a block of audio memory that another thread had just freed. It now holds the heap's lock while it writes. The crash it fixes elsewhere hasn't been seen here.
- **Slow state: a strong sign, cause still open.** About a third of the runs at the prison and at the boat get slower partway through and stay slow. The work is the same: the game's own GPU time a frame rises only a little (about 146 against 123 ms), but each GPU submission that waits takes 3 to 4 times longer (about 20 against 5.4 ms), inside macOS's GPU submit call, so the GPU sits idle while we wait. The GPU driver's own counters show why: in every slow run several gigabytes of the game's GPU memory are allocated but not wired (4.6 to 10.7 GB at the boat, 3.2 to 9.6 GB at the prison), against 0.5 to 0.8 GB in every fast run (8 slow and 12 fast runs). First reading: macOS's collector of wired GPU memory takes back part of the game's 26 to 30 GB, and the driver wires it back at each submission. But a run with `VICTORY_GPU_BUDGET_MB=4096` stayed fast, and two runs on the Series S profile showed the largest gap (8.7 and 13.2 GB) and then ended in the game's watchdog crash, so the unwired memory may be a result of stalled submissions as much as their cause. It is not general memory pressure: compressed memory and page-ins don't differ, and giving up 6.5 GB of host copies didn't help (see "didn't help"). With that collector turned off (`sudo sysctl iogpu.disable_wired_collector=1`, until the next restart), 3 of 8 runs still went slow, so it is not the cause, or not the only one. A fixed 30 GB floor for wired memory (`iogpu.wired_lwm_mb=30720`) didn't prevent it either (3 of 10 slow): in slow frames the driver still trims the game's GPU memory to about 16 GB. It is not the ray tracers (`VICTORY_BVH_MISS=1` doesn't prevent it) and not 64-bit atomic locks left taken. Boat results take three runs a side.
- **Render scale reaches the render targets** (only with `VICTORY_RENDER_SCALE`; from a tester's patch in [issue #1](https://github.com/halfwave50/VIctory-macOS-halfwave50/issues/1)). The game's own upscaler kept its largest render size at 2/3 of the output, so a scale of 0.5 at 3840x2160 still made 1440p render targets. With `VICTORY_RENDER_SCALE` set, that limit now follows the asked scale too. The tester's prison cell went from 273 to 254 ms a frame (M5 Pro, 3840x2160 at 0.5); not measured here yet. Nothing changes without the switch, and if the game's code at that place differs, both changes are left out.
- **Wave reconverges after 64-bit atomic locks** (from VIctory for macOS #445, always on). After the rounds that take a 64-bit atomic's lock, the lanes of a wave come back together before going on. On an M1 Pro it ended the GPU aborts in the prologue ([issue #7](https://github.com/halfwave50/VIctory-macOS-halfwave50/issues/7)). No speed change here: the boat cutscene ran at 298 to 300 frames a minute after it (three runs).
- **Narrower water fix** (off by default). `VICTORY_HALVES_WHOLE_WAVE_LDS=<program list>` keeps the far-reflection fix's new barriers (below) to the ray programs only; unset, every program gets them. With it, `VICTORY_HALVES_WHOLE_WAVE=1` and the pass back in, four boat runs showed clean water, and the driving scene ran at 298 to 307 ms a frame (one run 432, in the slow state), the same as the defaults (297 to 308). It is in the launcher now (see "Far reflection pass back in").
- **Far reflection pass without blocks** (off by default). The ray-traced pass the launcher leaves out (`6d7fe7745ab9c6d8`) drew rectangular blocks on water for two reasons, both from running the console's 64-lane waves as two 32-lane halves: the programs that sort rays added up their values per half, so half of the ray list was never written, and the program that counts rays per screen tile let both halves update the same counters at once. `VICTORY_HALVES_WHOLE_WAVE=1` fixes both (sums over the whole wave, and barriers between the halves). With it and the pass back in (`VICTORY_SKIP_PIPELINES=` empty), no picture of the boat shows blocks, and the boat cutscene runs at 293 and 300 frames a minute, against 282 and 293 with the switch alone. It is a launcher default now. A dark band along the shoreline remains; it is there with the pass left out too.
- **Flicker fix back to `keep`.** For a few hours the launcher used `VICTORY_PLACED_AT_ONCE=all` (every rewritten texture slot made at once). It cost 16% in the boat cutscene and came with a rare game crash (8 of 59 runs, none in 28 with `=keep`). It also didn't remove the empty reads it aimed at: the textures it made filled the GPU texture budget, older ones were dropped, and shaders read those empty instead. `=keep` loses nothing on the picture and is the launcher default again. `=read` (make at once only slots a shader read lately) gave no gain and stays off. The empty reads left under `=keep` are textures new to the shot at a camera cut, empty for one GPU submission; pictures taken in such a burst show nothing missing.
- **Controller freezes in play** (on in the launcher, not tried with a pad yet). In one play run the pad stopped responding: Wine's XInput stopped getting new readings while the pad stayed connected. `VICTORY_PAD_REARM=1` restarts a pad read that stops (a held trigger stays held), and `VICTORY_PAD_RUMBLE=0` keeps the game's rumble from the pad: a rumble write over Bluetooth can hold the pad's lock with no time limit, the likeliest cause. The launcher also logs Wine's XInput and HID errors, so a next freeze names itself. `VICTORY_PAD_STALE_REST=<seconds>` (opt-in) reads a pad that stays off its rest position that long as at rest.
- **Water simulation.** One program of the shallow-water simulation reads raw descriptor data as numbers. Our shader translator refused it, so the simulation ran without its time step. It now translates (`VICTORY_DESCRIPTOR_AS_DATA=1`, from ScarlettMac, a launcher default): at the boat the wake and spray show, at the same speed (240 against 244 frames a minute). The first run after the switch changes rebuilds shaders. `VICTORY_DESCRIPTOR_AS_DATA=0` turns it off.
- **Ray tracing, first steps (off by default).** The game's programs that build its ray-tracing structures now translate and run (`VICTORY_BVH_BUILDER=1`). At the boat, draws dropped for lack of a structure fell from about 340 to 8 a frame, with no hang or crash, but the GPU takes 25 to 30% longer. If the GPU stops a submission while the builder runs, the builder is switched off for the rest of the run. The picture doesn't gain yet. In one boat-cutscene run with the builder and the fixed far pass on (above), it kept 289 frames a minute; that run's pictures are not checked yet. `VICTORY_POINTER_PAGES` (off by default; idea from hyphennn's and DexNom's forks) gives game memory that rays reach only by address a copy after its first miss; with `=3` no shader writes are held back any more, but the boat cutscene runs at about 1.1–1.3 fps. The prison window doesn't reflect the player character on the console either, so our glass matches there. Ray tracing waits for more speed.
- **Two small speed-ups together.** `VICTORY_CENSUS_BATCH=1` (the remaining slot-list updates batched too) and `VICTORY_SHADER_OPT=64` (console loads read four values at a time, from 64bitMango's fork) were each under our 3% bar alone. Together, in blocks of three runs each, the prison cell went from 3.19 to 3.33 fps (+4.5%). Both are launcher defaults; set either to 0 to turn it off. The first run after `VICTORY_SHADER_OPT` changes rebuilds shaders.
- **Fill light at night.** The fill light's minimum level (0.3) won in nearly all of the night picture and flattened the lamps. With the stand-in off, the night street gets the console's red, teal and magenta light. The launcher now uses `VICTORY_AMBIENT_FLOOR=0.1`: part of the contrast comes back at night, and the prison cell and the boat stay lit.
- **One-frame black flickers.** When the game moves a texture to a new address and rewrites its shader slot, the slot stayed empty until a shader had read it once. Draws that picked it read zeros for one frame: black with white specks, about half a second on screen at 2 fps. `VICTORY_PLACED_AT_ONCE=keep` keeps the old texture in the slot until the new one is ready, and `=all` (no longer the default, see above) gives every rewritten slot its new texture at once, so new objects aren't black on their first frame either. In free roam, the log's count of slots read while being rewritten fell from 2,162 to 0; in the prison cell `=all` also took the empty first views from 5,839 to 0. No cost on the still view; the first minute after loading looks about 10% slower in our runs. A check by eye in free roam is still to come. `VICTORY_FLICKER_WATCH=1` logs such empty reads, and `VICTORY_PLACED_AT_ONCE=0` turns the fix off.
- **Exact hair shadow mask.** One hair-shadow program now keeps its shared memory in order across loop rounds (`VICTORY_HALVES_LDS_ROUNDS=1`, a launcher default). Hair looks the same or slightly cleaner, at no speed cost.
- **Screen reflections.** The game writes its reflection image through one texture and the lighting reads the same memory through a second view in another format. The port gave the two views separate GPU textures and copied nothing between them, so no reflection ever reached the picture: steel looked black and water reflected nothing. The view now uses the written texture (`VICTORY_PLACED_TWIN=1`), and a minimum reflection level of 0.15 gives reflections some light (`VICTORY_AMBIENT_REFLECT_FLOOR=0.15`). In the prison cell the steel box and plate get light; at the boat the water reflects the sky. One ray-traced reflection pass then drew rectangular blocks on the water. The launcher left it out (`VICTORY_SKIP_PIPELINES=6d7fe7745ab9c6d8`) until the blocks were fixed (see "Far reflection pass back in"). No speed cost. Both switches are launcher defaults.
- **Game speed for playing by hand.** `VICTORY_TIME_STEP_MAX=0.25` lets the game step its clock up to 0.25 s a frame, so the world runs closer to real time at low frame rates. A scripted walk in free roam went several times further. It is on in the launcher for playing; measured runs keep the game's own step. `VICTORY_TIME_STEP_MAX=` turns it off.
- **Fewer waits on one lock.** Threads that write GPU descriptors waited on a lock that guards the list of what each shader resource slot reads. The list is now updated in batches and checked once per barrier (`VICTORY_CENSUS_FAST=1`, a launcher default). Lock waits fell by half or more, and the prison cell got about 20% faster (no change at the boat). `VICTORY_CENSUS_FAST=0` turns it off.
- **Input for playing by hand** (on in the launcher, not yet tried in play; set any of them to 0 to turn it off):
  - `VICTORY_INPUT_LATCH=1`: the game reads the pad once a frame (250 to 500 ms here), so taps between two readings were lost. They are kept now.
  - `VICTORY_INPUT_REFOCUS=1`: keys held down while another app had focus wait for their release.
  - `VICTORY_STICK_DEADZONE=0.15`: a round dead zone on the sticks.
  - `VICTORY_INPUT_WATCH=1`: a log line a second with what the pad sends.
- **Zero-copy buffers.** Many of the game's buffers are now shared with the GPU instead of being copied every frame (`VICTORY_ALIAS_BUFFERS=1`, a launcher default). Copies fell from about 372 to 292 MB a frame, and the boat went from 257 to 280 frames a minute. It uses 1 to 2 GB more memory. `VICTORY_ALIAS_BUFFERS=0` turns it off.
- **Direct sunlight.** The lighting multiplies the sun by a texture that a compute pass writes. D3DMetal cannot store to that texture's format (B5G5R5A1), so the writes were lost and the sun came out black. The fix gives such textures a format that Metal can store (taken from Belobug's AMD fork).
- **Black bands in sunlit areas.** Four tile-list programs now run in their exact form (`VICTORY_HALVES_WALK=1`, a launcher default).
- **Fill light.** The console lights rooms by tracing rays. Here a stand-in estimates the light from the scene, with a minimum level so dark rooms don't go black (`VICTORY_AMBIENT=estimate`; `VICTORY_AMBIENT_FLOOR`, 0.3 in the code, 0.1 in the launcher).
- **Characters and clothes.** About 60 character pipelines had vertex data a little over one of Apple's size limits, so they were dropped without a warning. They draw now.
- **Blocky sun edges in the prison yard.** One shadow program asks a writable image for its size and gets 0, so whole 16x16 tiles were lit or shadowed together. `VICTORY_UAV_SIZE_LEVEL0=07f451a14b13fecb` gives that one program the right size. It is a launcher default now.
- **Tools.**
  - Frame replay plays back one recorded frame without the game, so GPU changes can be measured in seconds.
  - A watch on the request list.
  - A sampling profiler of the game's threads (`VICTORY_PROFILE`, from Belobug's fork). It now tells threads that wait inside Wine, on registry watches or on file reads from threads that run.
  - The per-frame log line also counts waits for the page-guard lock, time in memory-protection calls and the exceptions the write-fault handler sees.
  - `VICTORY_AB=<SWITCH>[=<value B>]:<seconds>` flips one run-time switch every few seconds while the game runs, so a single run compares A and B in the same scene. An A/A check came out at 0.0% (−0.3 to +3.0%), and turning off a switch with a known cost (`VICTORY_BATCH_CLAMPS`) came out 36% slower, as expected, so one run now answers for a run-time switch.
  - `VICTORY_KEYS=menu:<key>` presses a key at the start screens when the frames show them, instead of at fixed times, so a slow boot no longer misses them.
  - Optional switches that are off by default: `VICTORY_BVH_MISS` (from trustno1-coder's fork), and the ones listed below.
  - New optional switches, off by default and not measured yet:
    - From VIctory for macOS: `VICTORY_WIDENED_IN_PLACE` (no copy of a list's history at barriers; only two such views are live in our runs), `VICTORY_SNAPSHOT_SPANS` (write snapshots copy only new bytes), `VICTORY_UNMAPPED_SPLIT` (texture uploads), `VICTORY_WIDENED_COPY_SOURCE` (#586: widened texture copies get their source state back after shaders write them), and `VICTORY_CROSS_PAGE_READS=1` (#595: a read of game memory that crosses onto the next page reads that page's copy).
    - From ScarlettMac: `VICTORY_TEXTURE_TRIM`.
    - From Native-Victory-macOS: `VICTORY_DSTORAGE_ORDER=1` (held file reads keep the order they were asked in) and `VICTORY_CODE_TABLE_LOADS=1` (buffer loads around a shader's constant table read it).
    - Ours: `VICTORY_FREE_LOCKS=1` (frees 64-bit atomic locks after any submission where lanes gave up waiting for them), and `VICTORY_FLIGHTS_WITH=alias,writes,uploads` (a texture upload holds back the next GPU submission only when it meets what the last one may write). With `uploads` it was slower in a first prison run (176 frames a minute against 185 to 192): it looks at every texture. `uploads2` looks only at textures that changed and is being measured (286 frames a minute in one boat-cutscene run).

## What we tried that didn't help

Runs vary a lot, so some of these may get a second look.

- `VICTORY_OPENED_FAULTS`, `VICTORY_VETTED_PAGES` and `VICTORY_WATCH_RUNS` together (fewer lock waits and Wine calls when memory pages are guarded; two ideas from 64bitMango's fork): 104 and 148 frames a minute at the cell against 136 and 152.
- `VICTORY_HALVES_LDS_ORDER=1` (an exact order for one hair-shadow program's shared memory): 2% slower.
- `VICTORY_ASYNC_SETTLE_ALL=0`: no change, so it stays on.
- `VICTORY_DSTORAGE_WORKERS=6` (six file readers instead of one a core, from ScarlettMac): slower in the opening, 156 and 157 frames a minute against 173 and 171.
- `VICTORY_UNMAPPED_SPLIT`, `VICTORY_DIRTY_ROWS` and `VICTORY_HEAP_WORKER` together (texture uploads, from VIctory for macOS): the game crashed in both runs, about 3 minutes in. One run on the default settings later crashed at the same place, so it is a rare crash that was already there. Alone, `VICTORY_DIRTY_ROWS=1` was faster once and then slower in repeats (3.19 against 3.44 fps); it stays off.
- `VICTORY_VIEW_INLINE=1` (constant buffer views kept out of the view table, from hyphennn's fork): about 10% slower in the prison cell.
- `VICTORY_SYNC_EPOCH=1` (from ScarlettMac): slower in the cell (3.06 against 3.36 fps). With `VICTORY_TEXTURE_WORKERS=6` and `VICTORY_DEPTH_NO_TWIN=1` added: 3% slower.
- `VICTORY_PLACED_AT_ONCE=all`: 16% slower in the boat cutscene and a rare crash (see above).
- `VICTORY_FLUSH_OVERLAPPING=1` (a title copy refreshes only what it can change, from VIctory for macOS): +2.9% in the cell, under our 3% bar, and 0.0% in a one-run A/B.
- `VICTORY_UI_SIZE` (another size for the game's largest display mode): no visible effect.
- `VICTORY_BVH_MISS=1` (every ray-tracing node test misses): the blocks on water stayed, and 1 of 4 boat runs still went into the slow state.
- `VICTORY_COALESCE_PIECES=1` (the ready work of every queue goes to the GPU in one submission, from VIctory for macOS): host submissions fell from 31 to 27 a frame in the boat's driving scene, but the frame stayed the same (296 and 321 ms against 308).
- `VICTORY_UAV_SIZE_LEVEL0=1` (the image-size fix for every program instead of one): faces are clean now, but most runs went into the game's slower mode. It stays on one program.
- `VICTORY_FLIGHTS` gave no gain: the waiting just moved somewhere else.
- `VICTORY_FLIGHTS_WITH=alias[,writes]` (the next GPU submission prepared while the last runs, now also with zero-copy buffers): a few percent faster than `VICTORY_FLIGHTS=1` alone, but level with the defaults: 287 to 302 frames a minute in the boat cutscene against 287 and 290, and the same range in the prison cell.
- `VICTORY_RELEASE_COPIES=host` (idle host copies of game memory given up when macOS runs low on free memory, from VIctory for macOS #381 and #456): 6.3 to 6.7 GB less memory, but slower in the boat's driving scene (330 to 419 ms a frame against 296 to 310), and 2 of 3 runs still went into the slow state.
- `VICTORY_TABLE_MEMO=1` (a descriptor table already prepared isn't prepared again while nothing it uses changed, from VIctory for macOS #462): level with the defaults, 166 against 162 frames a minute in the prison cell; one other run with it ended in the rare watchdog crash.
- `VICTORY_DISPATCH_CLAMPS` cut clamp dispatches from 993 to 141 a frame but was no faster (139 frames a minute against 144 and 148; +2.1% in a one-run A/B).
- `VICTORY_PLACED_AT_ONCE=read`: no gain (175 against 191 frames a minute in the cell, 298 against 287 in the boat cutscene, one run each).
- `VICTORY_COPY_BACK_SPLIT` left copying back at 42 ms a frame (146 frames a minute).
- `VICTORY_TIME_STEP_MAX=1` breaks physics at the boat. 0.25 keeps the boat whole and is the launcher's value.
